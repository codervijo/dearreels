There's a strange moment in adult life when you realise your parents were
once people your age, uncertain and ambitious and making it up as they
went. Most of us know the outline of their lives and very little of the
inside.

These questions are for both parents, together or apart. Some work best
asked to one of them alone; some are more fun asked to both, so they can
correct each other's versions. For lists aimed at one parent, see
[questions to ask your mom](/questions/to-ask-your-mom/) and
[questions to ask your dad](/questions/to-ask-your-dad/).

## A few things that help

- **Ask each of them separately sometimes.** Two people's memories of the
  same event are rarely the same, and the differences are part of the story.
- **Don't fill the silence.** A pause after a good question is usually
  someone deciding how much to tell you.
- **Write down what surprises you.** Those are the threads to come back to.
- **Spread it out.** One question a week is easier on everyone than fifty in
  an afternoon.

## Before they were parents

1. Where did each of you grow up, and what was it like?
2. What were you like as teenagers?
3. What did you want to do with your lives back then?
4. What music were you listening to at twenty?
5. Who were your closest friends?
6. What was your first job?
7. What's something you did when you were young that you've never told us?
8. What did your own parents expect of you?

## How they met

9. How did you meet?
10. What did you each think of the other at first?
11. What was your first proper date?
12. When did you know?
13. What did your families think?
14. What was your wedding day like, beyond the photographs?
15. Where was your first home together, and what was in it?

## Raising us

16. What was the moment you found out you'd be parents?
17. What do you remember about the first year with a baby?
18. What was harder than you expected?
19. What did you disagree about when it came to raising us?
20. What did you do differently from your own parents, on purpose?
21. What's a story about us as children we've never heard?
22. What was the best family holiday, in your memory?
23. What did you sacrifice that we didn't notice?

## Work and home

24. How did you decide where to live?
25. What did your work mean to you?
26. Was there a time things were financially hard?
27. What did you do to unwind when we were young?
28. Which friends were part of our family's life back then?

## Family history

29. What do you know about your grandparents' lives?
30. Where did our family come from, as far back as you know?
31. Which family traditions do you want to keep going?
32. Is there a family recipe someone should learn?
33. Which relative should I get to know better while I can?

## Now

34. What does a good week look like for you now?
35. What are you each enjoying these days?
36. What's something you'd like to do together that you haven't yet?
37. What do you worry about lately?
38. How can I be more helpful from where I am?
39. What do you miss about when we lived at home?

## What they've learned

40. What do you know now that you wish you'd known at thirty?
41. What's the best decision you ever made?
42. What would you do differently?
43. What makes a marriage work, in your experience?
44. What are you most proud of?
45. What's the best advice your own parents gave you?
46. What do you hope your grandchildren learn from you?
47. When were you happiest as a family?
48. What do you want us to remember about you?
49. What's something you've always wanted to tell us?
50. Is there a question you're surprised nobody has asked you?

## The habit, not the interview

Asking all of this in one visit would be exhausting for everyone. The
answers that matter tend to come slowly, and often by surprise, days after
the question.

[Dearreels](/#how) turns this into a weekly rhythm. One question goes to your
parent on WhatsApp each week, in their own language. They answer when they
like, by voice note, video or a few lines. Every reply is gathered into a
private archive for the family. If your parents are getting older and you
feel the time narrowing, see
[questions to ask your aging parents](/questions/to-ask-aging-parents/).
