You probably know your mother better than almost anyone does, and still
there are whole rooms of her life you've never been inside. Who she was at
nineteen. What she gave up. What she'd do differently. The things she never
mentions because nobody has asked.

These questions are for opening those rooms. They work on a long call, at
the kitchen table, or one at a time in a message. Don't treat them as a
checklist. One good question, asked properly and followed with "tell me
more", is worth twenty ticked off a list.

## A few things that help

- **Ask one at a time.** A question a week will get you more than an
  interview ever does. People open up when it isn't a performance.
- **Follow the detail, not the list.** If she mentions a name you don't
  know, ask about the name. That's usually where the story is.
- **Let her answer in her own language.** Many mothers tell their best
  stories in the language they grew up speaking, even if you usually talk in
  another.
- **Voice beats text.** If she's comfortable sending voice notes, the way she
  tells a story is worth keeping as much as the story itself.

## Her childhood

1. What's your earliest memory?
2. What did the house you grew up in look like? Walk me through it.
3. What did your mother cook that you've never been able to recreate?
4. Who were you closest to as a child?
5. What were you scolded for most often?
6. What did you want to be when you grew up?
7. What games did you play, and who did you play them with?
8. Was there a place you went to be alone?
9. What were festivals or holidays like when you were small?
10. What's something from your childhood that doesn't exist anymore?

## Her own mother and family

11. What was your mother like when you were young?
12. What's something your mother used to say that you still hear in your head?
13. What do you know about how your parents met?
14. Which relative did you love visiting, and why?
15. What did your family argue about?
16. What's something your mother did for you that you only understood later?
17. Is there a family story everyone tells, the one that gets retold at every gathering?
18. Which of your grandparents do you remember most clearly?

## The years before you

19. What were you like at twenty?
20. Who was your closest friend when you were young, and where are they now?
21. What was your first job, and what did you do with your first salary?
22. What was the bravest thing you did before you had children?
23. How did you meet Dad? What did you first think of him?
24. What did your wedding day actually feel like — not the ceremony, the small things?
25. Was there a road you didn't take? Do you ever wonder about it?
26. What was the first place you lived after you left home?
27. What song takes you straight back to your twenties?

## Becoming a mother

28. What do you remember about the day I was born?
29. What surprised you most about being a mother?
30. What was the hardest year of raising us?
31. What did you worry about that turned out not to matter?
32. Was there a moment you felt proud of yourself as a mother?
33. What did you hope we'd become? Did it change?
34. What did you have to give up, and do you miss it?
35. Who helped you most when we were small?

## About me

36. What was I like as a baby?
37. What's a story about me I've never heard?
38. When did you first notice I was becoming my own person?
39. What did I do that drove you mad?
40. What do you think I get from you?
41. What's something you've never told me because the moment never came up?
42. When did you last worry about me, and why?

## Her life now

43. What does a good day look like for you these days?
44. What are you enjoying right now that you didn't expect to?
45. Who do you talk to most during the week?
46. What's something you'd like to learn, even now?
47. What do you miss most about the place you used to live?
48. Is there anything you'd like more help with, but haven't asked for?
49. What makes you laugh lately?
50. If you could spend one ordinary day anywhere, where would it be?

## The bigger questions

51. What are you most grateful for?
52. What do you believe now that you didn't believe when you were young?
53. What's the best advice anyone ever gave you?
54. What would you tell your younger self?
55. What do you hope your grandchildren know about you?
56. When have you felt most at home?
57. What do you want to be remembered for?
58. What do you still want to do?
59. Is there anyone you've lost touch with that you think about?
60. What should I ask you that I haven't?

## Making it a habit

The hard part isn't finding questions; it's asking them. Weeks fill up, the
call ends up being about logistics again, and the question you meant to
ask waits for a better moment.
