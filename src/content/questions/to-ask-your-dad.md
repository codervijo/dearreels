Many fathers don't volunteer stories. Ask "how was your childhood?" and
you'll get "fine." Ask "what did your father do on Sunday mornings?" and you
might get twenty minutes you'll want to keep forever.

The trick with dads is specificity. The questions below ask about concrete
things like places, jobs, objects and decisions, because those are the
doors most fathers walk through more easily than feelings. The feelings
usually follow on their own.

## A few things that help

- **Ask about things, not feelings.** "What was your first car?" leads to
  more than "were you happy back then?"
- **Ask for his advice.** Many fathers who won't talk about themselves will
  talk at length when asked how to do something.
- **Give it time.** A question sent on a Monday may get its real answer on a
  Thursday, after he's thought about it.
- **One question at a time.** Ten questions in one call feels like a test. One
  a week feels like interest.

## Growing up

1. What did your father do for work, and what did you think of it as a boy?
2. What was the neighbourhood like where you grew up?
3. What did you do after school?
4. What did you get into trouble for?
5. Who was your hero when you were young?
6. What was the first thing you ever saved up money for?
7. What did your family do on Sundays or holidays?
8. What's a smell or sound that takes you straight back to childhood?
9. What was your favourite subject, and which teacher do you still remember?

## His own father

10. What was your father like?
11. What did he teach you, on purpose or by accident?
12. What did you and your father disagree about?
13. Is there something he said to you that stuck?
14. What do you understand about him now that you didn't then?
15. In what ways are you like him? In what ways did you decide not to be?

## Work and ambition

16. What was your first job?
17. How did you end up in the work you did?
18. What was the best boss you ever had, and what made them good?
19. What was the hardest decision you made at work?
20. Was there a job you turned down? Do you wonder about it?
21. What are you proudest of from your working life?
22. What did your work cost you?
23. If you'd done something else entirely, what would it have been?

## Big decisions

24. What's the biggest risk you ever took?
25. How did you decide to marry Mom?
26. Why did you settle where you did?
27. What's a decision you'd make differently?
28. Was there a moment you were really afraid?
29. What's a time money was tight, and how did you get through it?

## Being a father

30. What do you remember about the day I was born?
31. What did you worry about most when we were young?
32. What was the best part of being a dad when we were small?
33. Was there something you wanted to give us that you never had?
34. What's a time you were proud of me that I might not know about?
35. What did you get wrong, if you're honest?
36. What do you think I get from you?

## Practical wisdom

37. What's the most useful skill you know that I should learn?
38. How do you know when to fix something and when to replace it?
39. What should I know about money that nobody teaches?
40. How do you deal with someone you don't trust?
41. What's the right way to apologise?
42. What do you do when you can't sleep from worry?

## Now

43. What does an ordinary day look like for you?
44. What are you enjoying these days?
45. Who are your friends now, and how did you meet them?
46. What do you miss most?
47. Is there something you'd still like to do, or see, or learn?
48. What's on your mind lately that you haven't mentioned?

## Looking back

49. What are you most grateful for?
50. What do you believe now that you didn't at thirty?
51. What does a good life look like to you?
52. When have you been happiest?
53. What do you want your grandchildren to know about you?
54. What do you hope I remember about you?
55. What's a question you wish someone had asked you?

## Asking, week after week

Most of these questions won't be answered in one sitting, and that's fine.
The best answers often come in pieces: a short reply now, and three weeks
later a long voice note that starts with "I was thinking about what you
asked."
