Your grandparents grew up in a world that has mostly disappeared. The
streets have changed, the jobs are gone, and many of the people they
mention by first name are known to no one else alive. They hold the
longest memory your family has.

Grandparents are often happy to talk; they just aren't asked. These
questions are written for grandchildren of any age, and for parents helping
young children ask. They work well across a distance too: a question by
message, an answer by voice note.

## A few things that help

- **Start with places.** "What did your village look like?" is easier to
  answer than "tell me about your life."
- **Ask about objects.** Old photographs, a ring, a recipe, a piece of
  furniture: each one carries a story.
- **Let them use their language.** If they grew up speaking a different
  language from yours, their best stories are probably in it. Ask anyway; you
  can find a translation later.
- **Bring the grandchildren in.** Young children asking simple questions often
  get answers adults never do.

## The world they grew up in

1. Where were you born, and what was the place like?
2. What did your house look like? Who lived in it?
3. What did you eat on an ordinary day? On a special day?
4. How did you get to school?
5. What did children do for fun?
6. What things did you have that children today wouldn't recognise?
7. What were the big events everyone talked about when you were young?
8. What was the first time you saw a television, a telephone, or a car?
9. What language did you speak at home, and with your friends?

## Their parents and family

10. What were your parents like?
11. What did your father and mother do for work?
12. How many brothers and sisters did you have? Who were you closest to?
13. What were your grandparents like? Do you remember them?
14. What family traditions did you grow up with?
15. What's a family story you heard as a child?

## Growing up and leaving home

16. What did you want to be when you grew up?
17. What was your first job?
18. When did you leave home, and why?
19. What was it like to move to a new place?
20. What did you do with your first money?
21. Who was your closest friend when you were young?

## Love and marriage

22. How did you meet Grandma / Grandpa?
23. What did you think of each other at first?
24. What was your wedding like?
25. Where did you live when you were first married?
26. What's the secret to staying together so long, if there is one?

## Raising a family

27. What was my mom / dad like as a child?
28. What was the funniest thing they did when they were small?
29. What was hard about raising children back then?
30. What was a typical day like when your children were young?
31. What did you hope your children would become?

## Hard years

32. What's the hardest time you lived through?
33. How did your family get through it?
34. Was there a time you had to start over?
35. What did those years teach you?
36. Who helped you when you needed it?

## Things they know

37. What's a recipe you'd like someone in the family to learn?
38. What's a skill you have that's disappearing?
39. What's a song or a prayer you've known all your life?
40. What should I know about our family's history?
41. Is there a place that matters to our family that I should visit one day?

## For grandchildren to ask

42. What was I like as a baby?
43. What's your favourite thing we've done together?
44. What were you like when you were my age?
45. What games did you play when you were little?
46. What makes you happy?

## What they want you to know

47. What are you most proud of?
48. What advice would you give me for my life?
49. What do you want our family to always remember?
50. What would you like to tell me that you've never had the chance to?

## When you live far away

video calls are short and noisy, with the toddler wandering off-screen.
