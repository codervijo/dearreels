Storyworth is a well-known service, and a good one: a question arrives each
week, your parent writes or records an answer, and after a year the stories
are printed as a hardcover book. If that sounds right for your family, it
probably is.

But some families find it doesn't quite fit, especially when the parent
lives in another country, is more comfortable speaking than writing, or
thinks in a language other than English. This page explains the
differences plainly, so you can choose the one that suits the person
you're buying for.

## How each one works

**Storyworth**, as described on its website (checked October 2026), sends
weekly questions by text or email. The recipient can reply by email, write
on the website, or record their voice over the phone. Guided phone
interviews and speech-to-text transcription are listed as upgrades. After a
year of writing, the stories become a premium hardcover book. Plans on
the site are shown "starting at" $59–$69, and a 30-day refund is offered.
Check [storyworth.com](https://www.storyworth.com/) for current details.

**Dearreels** sends one question a week on WhatsApp, the app many
parents, especially overseas, already use every day. Every prompt is
written by a person. Your parent can reply by voice note, video, photo or
text, in any language or mix of languages. At the end of the year, the
family receives a private digital archive, organised by week, with every
voice note, video and photo, plus a hand-bound printed transcript book. It's
$149 a year for one parent, with family members able to share the archive
through their own private links, and a full refund in the first 30 days.

## When Storyworth is the better choice

- Your parent enjoys writing and would love a printed book of their own
  words above all else.
- They're comfortable with email or text and English.
- You want the lowest-cost option, and the book is the point.

## When Dearreels fits better

- **Your parent lives far away and already lives on WhatsApp.** There's
  nothing new to learn: no app, no login, no website.
- **They'd rather talk than write.** Many parents say far more in a
  two-minute voice note than they ever would in an email. Keeping their
  actual voice, accent and laughter is often what families value most.
- **Their best stories are in another language.** Prompts can be sent in
  Hindi, Tamil, Malayalam, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Spanish,
  Portuguese, Arabic and more, and replies can mix languages freely.
- **You want to stay close now, not only to keep something later.** The
  weekly reply is the point. You hear something new from your parent every
  week, not just once a year when the book arrives.
- **Siblings are spread across countries.** Everyone in the family can open
  the same archive with their own private link.

## A question to ask yourself

Picture your parent on an ordinary morning. Are they more likely to sit
down and write a few paragraphs, or pick up their phone and talk for five
minutes in the language they grew up in? The answer usually tells you which
service they'll actually use, and a service they use is worth more than
one with a better book.
